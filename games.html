YOUR OWN LIBRARY, YOUR OWN DISCS

Emulators themselves are legal software — that was settled in the US courts decades ago (Sony v. Connectix, Sony v. Bleem). What matters is where the game files come from. If you own the disc or cartridge, you're in a defensible place. If you download a stranger's copy, you're not, and no seller can make that risk go away by bundling it for you.

The pistol behaves as a standard mouse-class pointer, so any emulator or PC game that accepts mouse aiming can use it. That's the whole trick — and why the library keeps growing without us shipping a single byte.

And it comes with one game we actually made: THE RANGE, the shooting gallery on our front page. Free, playable in your browser right now, no hardware needed — and if you score 2,500 it hands you a discount code.

WHAT ELSE IS OUT THERE

The PC light-gun scene is bigger than any pre-loaded drive, because it keeps being added to. Places worth your time:

WhereWhat you'll find
Steam, tagged "light gun" / "rail shooter" Modern and remastered rail shooters, plus indies built specifically for mouse-aim play. Filter by "Local Co-Op" if you bought the two-gun set.
Official retro compilations Publisher-released arcade collections put licensed classics on PC legitimately. When a compilation includes a shooting-gallery title, your pistol handles it like any other mouse-aim game.
itch.io & homebrew Independent and freeware light-gun games, some built by the same people who build the guns. Free to download, legal to keep.
The Gun4IR / OpenFIRE / Sinden communities The real authority on what works. These communities maintain per-game compatibility notes and settings — the fastest way to get a specific game feeling right, and they'll answer questions from anyone running compatible hardware.

Straight talk about "600 games." The drives you'll see advertised alongside guns like this are 1TB SSDs full of other people's console and arcade releases. Here's the part nobody mentions: in the current memory market, a bare 1TB SSD costs about as much as those loaded drives sell for. You're not paying for games — you're paying market rate for a hard drive, and getting a legal problem thrown in. Buy an empty drive, put your own library on it, and you've spent the same money with none of the exposure.
