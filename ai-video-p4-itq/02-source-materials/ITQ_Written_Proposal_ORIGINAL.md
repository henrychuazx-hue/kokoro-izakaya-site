## 4. Approach to the story flow

The narrative device is a **freeze-frame reveal**. A child absorbed in his phone walks into the blind spot of a reversing delivery van. Time freezes. A calm guardian appears: an anthropomorphic zebra, a nod to the zebra crossing and to "look both ways." He does not rescue the boy. He reveals the danger the boy never saw, time resumes, and **the boy saves himself**. The van passes, the boy reaches the basketball court he was rushing to, and catches the guardian's wink from a distance.

The guardian reveals; the child acts. This models safety as the child's own responsibility (保护自己，是自己的责任), which is the exact message WSHC wants a P4 audience to absorb. The freeze gives a child-friendly way to slow a dangerous moment down and teach inside it, with 一停 (stop), 二看 (look), 三行动 (act) mapped one-to-one onto the beat structure. The pacing is built for a nine-year-old: a relatable goal (the basketball game), a near-miss with real stakes but no trauma, a moment of wonder at the freeze, and a resolution the child earns himself and the audience can imagine performing.

The guardian is designed as a recurring series device, with one signature beat per scene and a wink-from-a-distance ending every episode: a mascot the curriculum can carry across future safety topics.

Language stays anchored to the provided WSHC CN script, kept P4-appropriate in vocabulary and sentence length. On-screen Chinese text (e.g. the opening message 「球赛开始啦！就等你！」) is reserved for moments that reinforce reading.

## 5. Approach to the 3D AI animation at the storyline highlights (requirement iv detail)

Three highlights carry the teaching load, and each is animated so the visual does the teaching:

**Highlight 1 — The freeze.** At the moment of danger the world stops. The van holds mid-reverse, dust hangs in the air, and only the camera moves. For a young viewer this is the spectacle beat, and it is also where the lesson happens: because time is frozen, the video can spend twenty seconds inside a hazard that in real life lasts under two, letting the guardian walk the boy and the audience through 一停、二看、三行动 without any sense of lecture. Technically, the frozen world is animated from locked frames with camera-only motion, so the moment reads as staged cinema.

**Highlight 2 — The blind-spot visualisation.** The guardian does not tell the boy about the blind spot; the animation shows it. The camera traces the sightline cut by the protruding vehicle, the driver's view blocked by stacked cargo, and the boy's position, invisible from the cab. A P4 pupil who has seen this sequence can reconstruct 盲点 spatially the next time they walk past a parked van, which is retention a spoken warning cannot produce.

**Highlight 3 — The wink.** The resolution belongs to the boy: he stops, checks and crosses himself, and the guardian's distant wink closes the episode with warmth. What makes this land is character consistency. One boy and one guardian, recognisably themselves in every one of the fourteen shots, produced by the continuity system in §7.

The whole film uses a painterly 3D style, a warm painted finish closer to feature animation than to television cartoon flatness. It stays bright and appealing to children while giving the safety beats real visual weight: the red reversing lights, the frozen dust, the gold shimmer of the freeze.

## 7. Production methodology

Our pipeline is a locked, ordered workflow. A visual breakdown of the process, from script to finished motion, is provided as **[Annex — Process Breakdown PDF]**.

**Step 1 — Anchor everything to a Master Plate.** Before any character is placed, we establish one definitive "world" frame: the location, its exact vehicle layout, light direction and geography. Every subsequent shot is generated from that plate, so the world never re-invents itself between cuts.

**Step 2 — Build a Cast & Asset Bible.** Every recurring element (hero, guardian, vehicles, interiors) is designed once as a reference sheet and reused, defined down to wardrobe and palette.

**Step 3 — Structured shot specification.** Each shot is authored as a structured build specification covering style, lighting, camera, subjects and continuity rules. The world, style and axis blocks are held identical across shots; only the camera and action change. This is the single biggest reason the shots cut together.

**Step 4 — Generate and QA shot frames.** Every frame is checked against written continuity laws: screen direction, camera side, light direction, prop states, character consistency, text accuracy. A failed frame is re-specified and regenerated from the specification. We do not patch over errors, because a patched frame carries its flaw into every later revision.

**Step 5 — Motion conversion.** Each locked frame drives its animated shot. Freeze beats are camera-only motion with the world held still.

**Step 6 — Assembly and post.** Clips are cut in shot order, scored, and laid against the human child Mandarin voice-over and the designed sound beats.

Because the plate, bible and style specification are reusable, later scenes and future curriculum topics come faster and cheaper than the first.

## 8. Review & QA workflow — built for three rounds

1. **Storyboard lock (client round 1).** All shot frames presented as a numbered board. The client returns numbered notes; we regenerate only what is flagged, anchored to the plate, until the board is approved.
2. **Motion lock (client round 2).** Animated clips reviewed per shot for movement, timing and continuity before assembly.
3. **Final lock (client round 3).** Full cut reviewed with voice-over, sound and music; final notes actioned.

Because revisions regenerate from specification, a round-2 note cannot silently break round-1 continuity, and each round stays fast and cheap compared with traditional CG re-rendering.
