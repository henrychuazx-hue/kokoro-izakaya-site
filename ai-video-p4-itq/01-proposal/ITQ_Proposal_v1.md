## 5. Approach to the story flow

The narrative device is a **freeze-frame reveal**. A child, absorbed in the moment, walks toward a hazard they cannot see. **Time freezes.** A calm guardian appears — an **anthropomorphic zebra**, a nod to the zebra crossing and to "look both ways." He does **not** rescue the child; he **reveals the danger**, time resumes, and **the child saves themselves**.

The guardian reveals; the child acts. This models safety as the child's **own responsibility** (保护自己是自己的责任) — exactly the message WSHC wants a P4 audience to absorb. The freeze is a child-friendly way to slow a dangerous moment down and teach *inside* it, with **一看 (look), 二想 (think), 三行动 (act)** mapped one-to-one onto the beat structure. The pacing is built for a nine-to-eleven-year-old: a relatable everyday goal, a near-miss with real stakes but **no trauma**, a moment of wonder at the freeze, and a resolution the child earns.

The guardian is designed as a **recurring series device** — one signature beat per scene and a **wink-from-a-distance** ending — a mascot the curriculum can carry across future safety topics.

Language stays anchored to the provided WSHC CN script, kept P4-appropriate in vocabulary and sentence length, and deliberately uses the target **curriculum vocabulary** (救护车、救护人员、包扎伤口、摩托车骑士、遵守交通规则、见义勇为、民防部队、「马路如虎口，你一定要小心！」) and the sentence pattern 「一个人⋯⋯，另一个人⋯⋯」. On-screen Chinese text is reserved for moments that reinforce reading.

## 6. Approach to the 3D AI animation at the storyline highlights (requirement iv detail · 15%)

Three highlights carry the teaching load, and each is animated so the **visual does the teaching**:

**Highlight 1 — The freeze.** At the moment of danger the world stops: a vehicle holds mid-motion, dust hangs in the air, and only the camera moves. For a young viewer this is the spectacle beat — and it is where the lesson happens, because time is frozen, so the film can spend fifteen–twenty seconds inside a hazard that in real life lasts under two, walking the child and audience through **一看、二想、三行动** with no sense of lecture. Technically, the frozen world is animated from **locked frames with camera-only motion**, so the moment reads as staged cinema.

**Highlight 2 — The blind-spot / hazard visualisation.** The guardian does not *tell* the child about the danger; the animation *shows* it — the sightline cut by a reversing vehicle, a driver's view blocked by cargo, the child invisible from the cab; or the phone-screen bubble that hides an oncoming rider. A pupil who has *seen* this can reconstruct the hazard spatially next time — retention a spoken warning cannot produce.

**Highlight 3 — The wink.** The resolution belongs to the child: they stop, check and cross safely, and the guardian's distant wink closes with warmth. What makes it land is **character consistency** — one hero and one guardian, recognisably themselves in every shot, produced by the continuity system in §8.

The whole film uses a **painterly 3D style** — a warm painted finish closer to feature animation than to flat TV cartoon — bright and appealing to children while giving the safety beats real visual weight: the red reversing lights, the frozen dust, the gold shimmer of the freeze. This is **more sophisticated than the 2D sample** referenced in the brief, as required.

## 8. Production methodology

Our pipeline is a **locked, ordered workflow**. A visual breakdown (script → finished motion) is provided as the process diagram in the deck.

1. **Anchor everything to a Master Plate.** Before any character is placed, we establish one definitive "world" frame — location, vehicle layout, light direction, geography — so the world never re-invents itself between cuts.
2. **Build a Cast & Asset Bible.** Every recurring element (hero, zebra guardian, vehicles, interiors) is designed once as a reference sheet and reused, defined down to wardrobe and palette.
3. **Structured shot specification.** Each shot is authored as a structured build spec (style, lighting, camera, subjects, continuity rules). World/style/axis blocks are held identical across shots; only camera and action change — the single biggest reason the shots cut together.
4. **Generate & QA shot frames.** Every frame is checked against written continuity laws: screen direction, camera side, light direction, prop states, character consistency, and **Chinese text accuracy**. A failed frame is re-specified and regenerated — we do not patch, because a patched flaw propagates into every later revision.
5. **Motion conversion.** Each locked frame drives its animated shot; freeze beats are camera-only motion with the world held still.
6. **Assembly & post.** Clips are cut in shot order, scored, and laid against the **human child Mandarin voice-over** and designed sound beats, then graded and mastered to the required delivery formats.

Because the plate, bible and style spec are reusable, later scenes — and future curriculum topics — come **faster and cheaper** than the first.

## 9. Review & QA workflow — built for three rounds

1. **Storyboard lock (client round 1).** All shot frames presented as a numbered board with the EN storyboard + CN script. WSHC returns numbered notes; we regenerate only what is flagged, anchored to the plate, until endorsed.
2. **Motion lock (client round 2).** Animated clips reviewed per shot for movement, timing and continuity before assembly.
3. **Final lock (client round 3).** Full cut reviewed with voice-over, sound and music; final notes actioned.

Because revisions regenerate **from specification**, a round-2 note cannot silently break round-1 continuity, and each round stays fast and cheap versus traditional CG re-rendering. *(Reviews/edits beyond three rounds are quoted in Annex C item 5.)*
