## 5. Approach to the story flow

The narrative device is a **freeze-frame reveal**, carried by a recurring **zebra guardian** — an anthropomorphic zebra, a nod to the zebra crossing and to "look both ways," rendered as a **cinematic, understated** presence rather than a childish mascot, to hold the 11–12 maturity level and stay "more sophisticated than the 2D sample." He does **not** rescue anyone; he **reveals** the danger a character cannot see. Time resumes, and **a child acts** — the lesson is that safety is a decision you make (**一看、二想、三行动**), and that sometimes the safe, brave act is to protect *someone else*.

The film is built as **three escalating everyday vignettes plus a warm closing beat**, following the WSHC script exactly:

1. **Car-park blind spot** — a boy, crouched to pick something up, is invisible to a reversing driver; an adult passer-by (the **Uncle** in our cast) stops the driver. *(The film's first act of **见义勇为**.)*
2. **Phone at the crossing** — our hero warns a phone-distracted teen to wait; a motorcyclist rushes past the moment they'd have stepped out.
3. **Dash against the light** — a runner beats the countdown; a turning car brakes hard; everyone is shaken but safe.
4. **Care for one another** — the guardian's distant **wink** closes on the dual lesson: protect yourself, look out for others, and in an emergency **拨打995** for the **民防部队 (SCDF)**.

The pacing is built for an **eleven-to-twelve-year-old**: relatable everyday goals, near-misses with **real stakes but no trauma — no collision or injury is shown on screen**, and Scene 3 resolves on relief and the lesson, not on fear. Each freeze is a moment of wonder that earns the teaching.

Language stays anchored to the provided WSHC CN script, kept P4-appropriate, and deliberately exercises the target **curriculum vocabulary and the sentence pattern 「一个人⋯⋯，另一个人⋯⋯」** — placed explicitly, scene by scene, in §5.2. On-screen Chinese text (e.g. 「马路如虎口，你一定要小心！」) is reserved for moments that reinforce reading. The guardian is designed to be **reusable for future WSHC/MOE safety topics** (all IP vesting in TAL) — value for money, not a franchise.

> **Casting note:** the WSHC Scene 1 script reads 女孩 (CN) but "a boy" (EN); **our completed sample scene animates the boy** (with an adult "Uncle" passer-by and the zebra guardian). Final gender casting will be confirmed with WSHC at storyboard round 1 and kept continuous with the sample.

## 6. Approach to the 3D AI animation at the storyline highlights (requirement iv detail · 15%)

Three **techniques** recur across the three scenes, and each is animated so the **visual does the teaching**:

**Technique 1 — The freeze.** At the moment of danger the world stops: a vehicle holds mid-motion, dust hangs in the air, and only the camera moves. It is the spectacle beat *and* where the lesson happens — because time is frozen, the film can hold the audience inside a hazard that in real life lasts under two seconds, and walk them through **一看、二想、三行动** with no sense of lecture. Technically, the frozen world is animated from **locked frames with camera-only motion**, so it reads as staged cinema. We budget the freezes so they fit the runtime: one "hero" freeze of ~12–15s in the blind-spot scene, and shorter reveals (~5–8s) in the others.

**Technique 2 — The hazard visualisation.** The guardian does not *tell* a character about the danger; the animation *shows* it — the sightline cut by a reversing vehicle, a driver's view blocked by cargo, a child invisible from the cab; or the phone-screen bubble that hides an oncoming rider. A pupil who has *seen* this can reconstruct the hazard spatially next time — retention a spoken warning cannot produce.

**Technique 3 — The wink.** The resolution belongs to the child who acts safely; the guardian's distant wink closes with warmth. What makes it land is **character consistency** — one guardian and a small recognisable cast, produced by the continuity system in §8.

The whole film uses a **painterly 3D style** — a warm painted finish closer to feature animation than to flat TV cartoon — bright and appealing to children while giving the safety beats real visual weight: the red reversing lights, the frozen dust, the gold shimmer of the freeze. This is **more sophisticated than the 2D sample** referenced in the brief, as required.

## 8. Production methodology

Our pipeline is a **locked, ordered workflow**. A visual breakdown (script → finished motion) is provided as the process diagram in the deck.

1. **Anchor everything to a Master Plate.** Before any character is placed, we fix the core environment as **Photoshop-anchored plates** — in our demo, **side, street-level and overhead** exteriors plus in-vehicle **dashboard and cargo** views — so the world never re-invents itself between cuts and corrections stay cheap.
2. **Build a Cast & Asset Bible.** Every recurring element is locked as a reference sheet and reused — in our demo, character sheets for the **Boy, Uncle and Zebra Mascot** (with **expression sheets** for the human characters) and a **van prop sheet** used across the scene, defined down to wardrobe and palette.
3. **Structured shot specification.** Each shot is authored as a structured build spec (style, lighting, camera, subjects, continuity rules). World/style/axis blocks are held identical across shots; only camera and action change — the single biggest reason the shots cut together.
4. **Generate & QA shot frames.** Every frame is checked against written continuity laws: screen direction, camera side, light direction, prop states, character consistency, and **Chinese text accuracy**. A failed frame is re-specified and regenerated — we do not patch, because a patched flaw propagates into every later revision.
5. **Motion conversion.** Each locked frame drives its animated shot; freeze beats are camera-only motion with the world held still.
6. **Assembly & post.** Sequences are edited, stitched and colour-corrected (mixing the best of multiple takes); **audio is kept on separate stems** so the human child Mandarin voice-over and sound beats can be tuned independently, then graded and mastered to the required delivery formats.

Because the plate, bible and style spec are reusable, later scenes — and future curriculum topics — come **faster and cheaper** than the first.

## 9. Review & QA workflow — built for the client's review rounds

The brief allows **up to three review rounds on the storyboard** (before endorsement) **and, separately, up to three review rounds on the video**. Our workflow is built for exactly that:

- **Storyboard reviews (up to 3).** All shot frames presented as a numbered board with the EN storyboard + CN script. WSHC returns numbered notes; we regenerate only what is flagged, anchored to the plate, until the board is **endorsed** — production does not begin until then.
- **Video reviews (up to 3).** Once the storyboard is endorsed, the animated cut is reviewed — per-shot motion, timing and continuity, then the full assembly with voice-over, sound and music — with numbered notes actioned each round.

Because revisions regenerate **from specification**, a later note cannot silently break earlier-approved continuity, and each round stays fast and cheap versus traditional CG re-rendering. *(Any reviews/edits beyond the included three-storyboard-plus-three-video rounds are quoted in Annex C item 5.)*
