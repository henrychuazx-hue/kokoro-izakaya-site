60-SECOND SETUP

Three steps, one calibration, then you're shooting. No drivers to hunt down, no soldering, no CRT required. If you can plug in a USB stick you can do this.

WHAT'S IN THE BOX

  • RetroTrigger™ pistol (per player)
  • USB receiver — plug & play
  • IR positioning light set
  • 2× 18650 li-ion cells per pistol
  • Charging cable per pistol
  • Quick-start & calibration card

You supply the host and the games. The pistol is a controller — it needs something to play on (a Windows PC, a Batocera/Raspberry Pi box, or an Android TV device) and games you own. See what you can play.

THE THREE STEPS

MOUNT THE IR BAR

Clip the positioning light to the top or bottom of your screen, centred, facing you. It draws power over USB — any port or phone charger works. This is what the pistol tracks; the screen itself does nothing.

≈ 20 SECONDS

PAIR THE RECEIVER

Insert the cells into the pistol grip, flick the switch at the rear, and plug the USB receiver into your host. The blue bars on the slide show charge. Pairing is automatic — each pistol ships matched to its own receiver.

≈ 20 SECONDS

CALIBRATE ONCE

Hold the trigger and aim at each corner of the screen as prompted. The pistol stores the geometry, so you only redo this if you move the bar or change screens.

≈ 20 SECONDS

Sitting distance matters more than screen size. Aim for roughly 1.5× to 2.5× your screen's width away from it — about 2–3m for a 55", 2.5–4m for a 75". Too close and the IR points spread past the pistol's field of view; too far and precision drops.

SET UP YOUR HOST

Windows PC — the path most people take

  1. Plug the receiver in. Windows detects the pistol as a standard pointing device — no driver download, no installer.
  2. Run the corner calibration (hold trigger, aim at each prompted corner).
  3. Open a game that accepts mouse aiming and set aim = mouse. Most PC rail shooters and arcade emulators do this natively.
  4. For emulators, bind the trigger to your usual fire button and the reload gesture (aim off-screen and pull) to the reload input. The light-gun community's compatibility lists are the fastest way to get per-game settings right — we link them on the games page.

Two players: plug both receivers in. Each pistol drives its own pointer, so P1 and P2 are independent — no split-screen, no extra software.

Batocera or Raspberry Pi — the cabinet builder's route

  1. Flash Batocera to a USB drive or SD card and boot your box. (A Pi 4/5, an old mini-PC or any spare laptop all work.)
  2. Plug in the receiver before boot so it's detected on startup.
  3. In Batocera, the pistol appears as a mouse-class device — enable light-gun mode for the emulator cores you use, then run calibration.
  4. Point Batocera at the games you legally own. Batocera ships no games, and neither do we.

Why builders like this: the whole thing lives on a stick. Unplug it from the TV, take it to a friend's house, plug in, calibrate once, play.

Android TV box, tablet or phone

  1. Connect the receiver via USB-OTG (a USB-C adapter for most modern devices).
  2. Android treats the pistol as a mouse — you'll see a pointer appear.
  3. Run calibration, then use an emulator front-end that supports mouse aiming.
  4. Android is the most variable host: pointer handling differs between devices and Android versions. If you only have an Android box and precision matters to you, tell us before you order and we'll be straight with you about what to expect.

IF SOMETHING'S OFF

My aim is offset — I hit above/below where I point

Re-run calibration. Nine times out of ten the IR bar moved, or it was calibrated at a different sitting distance than you're playing at. Calibrate from the seat you actually play in, not standing next to the TV.

Tracking drops out near the screen edges

You're likely too close, so the outer IR points fall outside the pistol's field of view. Back up half a metre and recalibrate. Also check the bar is centred — an off-centre bar makes one side worse than the other.

The pointer jitters or jumps around

Infrared interference. The usual culprits: sunlight on the screen wall, incandescent or halogen bulbs in the pistol's view, a plasma TV, or a second IR device (some soundbars and remotes). Close the curtains and try again in the dark to confirm — then remove the offender.

Recoil feels weak, or stops after a while

Check cell charge first — the solenoid is the first thing to fade on low voltage. Recoil pulls hard current, so tired or mismatched 18650s show up here before anything else. Fit a freshly charged matched pair.

Nothing happens when I plug in the receiver

Try a different USB port directly on the host (skip hubs and front-panel ports). Confirm the pistol's rear switch is on and the charge bars light. If you have two kits, swap receivers to identify whether it's the pistol or the receiver — then email us with what you found and we'll sort it.

A game ignores the pistol entirely

The game probably doesn't read raw mouse position for aiming — common in console-style games that expect a stick. Check the community compatibility list linked on the games page before assuming the hardware is at fault.

BATTERY & CARE

  • Charge with the supplied cable; a full charge takes a few hours and lasts a long session.
  • The cells are removable 18650s, so a spare pair means you never stop playing to charge. Use quality protected cells from a reputable brand — cheap unbranded cells are the one part of this hobby genuinely worth spending on.
  • Don't store the pistol with fully depleted cells, and don't leave it in a hot car. Standard lithium sense.
  • Wipe the muzzle lens occasionally — a smudge there reads as poor tracking.
